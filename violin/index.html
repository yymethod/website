YY Method™ / Applications / Violin

One music stand. Three different jobs for AI.

A violin education is carried by more than one person. The learner at the instrument. The adult between lessons. The teacher above them both.

The YY Method gives AI a different job — and a different boundary — in each of those seats. One application uses AI-generated story to make real practice consequential and creatively alive. One builds pitch, notation, and theory fluency on musical facts that can actually be verified. One helps the adult think clearly between lessons without becoming, or creating, a second teacher.

There is deliberately no app for the teacher's seat. That seat stays human.

Quick Answer

YY Method for Violin is one doctrine applied three ways. Music Practice RPG uses AI-generated story to make real practice consequential — without punishing missed days. Violin Pitch Builder builds pitch, notation, fingerboard, and theory fluency on verified musical material — the AI never grades a learner's recording. Violin for Parents helps the adult between lessons — without replacing the child's teacher. The three are designed to work together, and none of them replaces the teacher.

Pressure Test

Violin gives the YY Method three different kinds of pressure test.

One system asks AI to generate an evolving story around real practice. One asks it to adapt a structured pitch curriculum without inventing musical truth. One asks it to help parents reason between lessons without inheriting the authority of the teacher, clinician, luthier, child, or parent.

The boundaries therefore move. That is intentional. If the doctrine produced the same constraints regardless of the job, it would be a slogan, not a method.

Same doctrine. Different assignment of authority.

Every seat at the stand gets a different kind of help.

A learner needs a reason to come back tomorrow, a creative stake in their own playing, and skills that are actually true. An adult supporter needs judgment at 7pm on a Wednesday when the teacher isn't in the room. A teacher needs students who arrive having practiced — not students shaped by an app's competing opinions.

The learner's seat holds two applications because they solve different problems: one carries practice continuity, consequence, and story; the other carries pitch, notation, and theory fluency grounded in verified musical truth.

People ask which app is for the teacher. None of them. That's the whole point. - B.C.

The Three Applications

Choose by role, pressure, and boundary.

Music Practice RPG

For musicians 13+ who want real practice to feel consequential — and more creatively alive.

Real music practice changes a persistent fictional story. You practice your actual instrument, make choices only you would make, invent your own approaches, and return later to find out what your practice caused.

The boundary: the story may make you want to return. It may not make failure to return costly. Missed days cannot punish you, characters cannot guilt you, and better playing does not buy better story treatment.

See how the story is governed →

Violin Pitch Builder

For the violinist building pitch, notation, fingerboard, and theory fluency on musical facts that can actually be verified.

Your musical work earns the Bank Notes that repair and build a persistent village that keeps the history of what you did. Objective musical truth exists here, and the app checks it deterministically.

The boundary: AI adapts the village; it does not invent the music. It never listens to your recording and declares you sharp, flat, or improved, it cannot invent pitch truth or violin pedagogy — and your creative playing stays ungraded human territory.

See where AI stops →

Violin for Parents

For the adult supporting a learner — the only one of the three built for you, not the player.

Between lessons, it helps with the ordinary friction of a musical childhood — practice resistance, a forgotten instruction, a recurring stuck moment, deciding whether to step in or wait — by organizing what you observed, ruling out what doesn't fit, and preparing one useful next step or teacher question.

The boundary: it will not train you into a second teacher, become one itself, diagnose pain, clear an instrument as safe, or invent facts about your family.

Designed to work together. Start with one.

Here is what a week can look like when the three applications share a household with one violin student and one supporting adult.

The learner practices the teacher's actual assignment, and Music Practice RPG turns that real session into a choice with a consequence waiting tomorrow. On another day, ten minutes in Violin Pitch Builder turns pitch and notation work into a repaired building and a resident who remembers it. Midweek, the practice fight returns — the parent opens Violin for Parents, which helps sort what actually happened from what it might mean, and turns a frustrating evening into one specific question for Thursday.

At the lesson, those three kinds of support can converge in something useful to the teacher: a student who actually practiced, pitch and theory work grounded in verified musical material, and an adult who arrives with better observations and one good question instead of a guess.

The three applications never share the learner's authority between them, because none of them holds it. Practice motivation and creative continuity, verified skills, and adult judgment are different problems. Each app takes one and refuses the others.

Why this matters: the apps don't compete with each other for the same job — so adding a second one adds a capability, not a second opinion.

Start where the pressure is.

If practice itself is the issue — returning to it, caring about it, feeling like it leads somewhere — and the musician is 13 or older, start with Music Practice RPG. If the learner is younger, or the question is really "what should I do about the practice fights," start with Violin for Parents.

If the gap is pitch, reading, or theory — the ear, the page, the fingerboard — start with Violin Pitch Builder. It's the one place in this family where answers are checked against verified musical material rather than an AI's impression.

If the hard moments happen between lessons — practice resistance, uncertainty about what the teacher meant, a recurring stuck moment, or something physical or instrumental that may need a human — start with Violin for Parents. It is the only adult-facing application, and its everyday job is your judgment.

None of these choices locks anything out. The applications are independent, and each one works alone.

None of this replaces the teacher.

The teacher should meet these apps as support around their work, not as competing instruction — and each application supports that work differently.

Music Practice RPG can support more meaningful real practice: the teacher's actual assignments remain the true musical context, the app cannot override technique, sequence, or correction, and the student still decides what they actually practice today.

Violin Pitch Builder can strengthen pitch, notation, and theory fluency on verified material: it deliberately carries only that narrow slice and leaves repertoire, technique, interpretation, and individualized instruction where they belong.

Violin for Parents can improve what arrives from home: better parent observations and teacher-ready questions — with teacher authority as its central subject, so the parent is not trained into a second teacher, the AI does not become one, and an unavailable teacher does not transfer authority to an app.

Capability does not create authority. That rule holds in all three, and it is why a teacher can meet these applications without meeting a rival.

One loop underneath all three.

Every one of these applications runs on the same discipline the doctrine describes: Capture what actually happened before explaining it. Ask why the current explanation might be true — and why it might not be. Commit to one small, honest experiment. Preserve what you learned, including what stayed uncertain.

At a music stand, that sounds like: "Late every time, or only from one string?" beats "your shifting is bad." One useful question beats a premature diagnosis — whether the question comes from a story character, a village resident, or a coach helping a parent think.

Fluency is not authority. A generated sentence is not proof. In all three applications, AI may execute substantial work. It does not inherit the human judgment or domain authority underneath it.
